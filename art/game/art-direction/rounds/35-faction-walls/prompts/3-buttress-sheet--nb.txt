The sheet shows the rampart of a toy fortress built from chunky full-tile blocks on a board of square tiles of bright green grass, seen in a three-quarter view from well above, the way a board game is seen from a chair. Every block fills one whole square tile and stands about three quarters as tall as the tile is wide, so the rampart is a thick square-section wall with a flat stone walkway along its top and a grid of two by two square merlons, the teeth of a battlement, standing on each block's walkway. The stone is warm cream with crisp edges and narrow flat bevels, and blocks that share a side join into one continuous wall. One straight run of four blocks turns a corner and continues for three more blocks; the two blocks at the far end carry a second storey of the same block stacked on top, twice as tall, with merlons only on the upper storey. Below the run, smaller and alone on its own grass tile, stands one single block seen a little closer so its sides show. The look: a square pilaster strip stands proud at every block corner, from the ground up to a projecting string course band just under the walkway, so the run reads as a row of shallow buttresses with slightly recessed stone panels between them. The walkway and the merlons stay plain.
