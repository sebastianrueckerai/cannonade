You are judging six painted concept sketches of the rampart of a toy fortress for a cozy strategy game. The rampart is built from chunky full-tile blocks on a board of square tiles; each block is a thick square-section wall with a flat walkway and two-by-two square merlons on top, and side-by-side blocks join into one wall. Each sheet shows one surface treatment of the block's sides, painted as a run that turns a corner, with two blocks at the far end carrying a second storey, plus one single block alone.
The game camera looks down at 60 degrees from far away: a block's top is about 19 pixels deep on screen and its vertical face only 5 pixels tall. The player must tell which walls belong to them and which to the enemy at a glance.
For each sheet, numbered 1 to 6 in reading order: (a) name what the treatment is, (b) say whether it would still read when the vertical faces are 5 pixels tall and the tops 19 pixels, (c) list any fault against the brief: an open doorway instead of a blind arch, stones drawn as lines rather than carved, a tower instead of a stacked second storey, parts that protrude past the block's tile, anything that is not cream stone with a flat walkway and square merlons. Then rank the six by how well two of them, used by two opposing players, would tell the two players' walls apart from the game camera, and name the best pair. Sheet 6 shows the colour idea, walkway and merlons stained in a team colour: say whether that reads better than the shape differences.
Answer in plain prose, under 400 words.
