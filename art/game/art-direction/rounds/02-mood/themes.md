# Round 02: four candidate worlds

Phase A of [art-direction.md](../../../../docs/process/art-direction.md). Every candidate starts from the toy-harbour world frame of round 01 (D15) and from the references: a saturated but cosy palette, a readable grid, the River Towns level of detail, the age of sail with medieval elements (D11, D14). The candidates differ in what the world is and what it feels like, not in the render style. Six images per candidate in [round.json](round.json): a coast, the fortress from the game camera, the harbour at another time of day, a battle moment, a material study, a sky. The reference collage per candidate, `refs-<slug>.jpg`, shows which Steam shots fit it.

## 1. The toy harbour (`toy-harbour`)

A painted wooden toy fortress island on a sunlit table, a sheet of teal sea around it, toy ships with striped sails. The era is an age of sail merchant harbour with a stone keep and one medieval tower, as a toy maker would build it. Tone: cheerful, chunky, sunlit. In the build phase the player stacks painted blocks on a board game, friendly and quick. In the battle phase it is a fairground shooting gallery: pop, splash, a ship tips over like a toy, no wreckage stays, no fire. Motifs that repeat: striped sails, crenellations like teeth, barrels and crates, a flag on every tower, pebble beaches, round trees, fat bevels on every block. It is not a battlefield, and it is not an asset-pack island: the toy reading is deliberate, with paint-like colours and fat bevels. This is the round 01 pick carried on without change, so the other three have something to beat.

## 2. The lighthouse islands (`lighthouse-islands`)

A scatter of small fogged islands in a teal sea that fades to cream at the horizon, a lighthouse fort on the largest, ships that appear out of the haze. The era is the age of sail at a remote trading post: a lighthouse, a signal mast, pale wooden piers, a medieval round tower for the keep, pine and gorse rather than palms. Tone: calm, airy, remote. In the build phase the player settles a quiet island, every placement clear against the water. In the battle phase masts appear out of the fog, the cannon fire lights the mist orange for a moment, then the calm returns. Motifs that repeat: the lighthouse, fog banks, gulls, rope, lanterns, pale wooden piers, pine trees, rock shelves at the waterline, the checker grid on the land. It is not a tropical postcard, no palms and no parrots, and it is not dark: the fog is soft and bright. This is the A Monster's Expedition note from D15 taken as a whole world.

## 3. The market town at the river mouth (`river-town`)

A walled market town where a river meets the sea, half-timbered houses packed behind stone walls, orange tile roofs, a round tower with a conical roof, mills and quays, green fields on a grid. The era is late medieval into the early age of sail: the first bronze cannons on old stone walls, cogs and barges at the quay. Tone: homely, busy, storybook. In the build phase the player raises a town house by house, every house one colour block with a roof, and the walls grow around them. In the battle phase it is a siege from the water: the fight is at the wall and on the river, the town behind the wall keeps going, cannon smoke drifts over the roofs. Motifs that repeat: orange tile roofs, timber frames, round towers with conical roofs, water channels, mills, market cloth, stacked barrels, hedged green fields on a grid. It is not a grey castle and not an empty fortress: the town is the point and the walls protect it. This is River Towns and Foundation taken as a whole world.

## 4. The shipwright's island (`shipwright-yard`)

A fortified shipyard on a rock, where everything is being built: wooden cranes, slipways, scaffolds, rope walks and a forge, the cannons cast in sight of the walls. The era is the age of sail at its busiest, a naval dockyard: oak and iron, bronze guns, the first forge chimneys, so the way to bigger guns is visible but not taken yet (D16). Tone: inventive, busy, warm metal. In the build phase the yard is the game: timber piles, stone blocks and iron bars stack visibly into the guns the player builds, which gives the ingredient metaphor of D17 a home. In the battle phase the yard's own guns answer the fleet, sparks and smoke, the forge glow behind, the cranes keep swinging. Motifs that repeat: wooden cranes and pulleys, slipways, rope coils, scaffolding, stacked timber, stone blocks and iron bars, forge glow, copper roofs, brass gun barrels. It is not steampunk yet, no gears for their own sake, and it is not grimy: the yard is tidy and bright.

## What the pick decides

The world in one sentence, the era mix, the tone words and the motifs go into chapter 1 of [art_bible.md](../../art_bible.md). Phase B takes the picked moodboard and extracts three palettes from it. A merge is allowed, named in words: for example the lighthouse fog on the toy harbour.
