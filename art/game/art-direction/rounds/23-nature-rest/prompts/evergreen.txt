It is a painting, not a 3D render, not low poly, not a game screenshot. Cozy and friendly like a storybook toy, never grim. No people, no text, no labels, no numbers, no letters, no watermark.

The sheet shows three stylized conifer trees for a toy fortress game, side by side in one row, each standing on its own square grass tile one metre wide, seen in a three-quarter view from slightly above. The tiles lie loose on the cream paper like game pieces, their edges drawn as a slightly darker green line, so the size of each tree reads against its tile. Every conifer has the same shape: one tall soft tree of stacked rounded foliage tiers, three or four plump rounded layers rising to a soft point, on a very short stocky trunk, about three quarters of a tile wide and about one and a half tiles tall. The three conifers differ only in their colours: Left: deep spruce green, darkest at the bottom, a soft blue-green at the top. Middle: warm pine green, a dark forest green base and a lighter yellow-green top. Right: muted fir green, a grey-green base and a fresh green top. In front of the middle object, on its own tile, stands a small dark iron cannon on a low square plate that fills its tile: it is the size reference of the game. The conifer must read as clearly taller than the cannon.
