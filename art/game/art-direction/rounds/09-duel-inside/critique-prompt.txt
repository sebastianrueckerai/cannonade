You are an independent critic for a low-poly browser strategy game in the spirit of Rampart: castles on a tile grid shoot at each other with cannons. This phase decides the main painted 2D illustration of the store page, the picture without its title. Your job is faults, above all the cannons, not taste.

The owner's rules: each castle has exactly two cannons, the same size, standing on the open courtyard floor inside the walls; never on a tower, never on a wall, never on a roof or a house; each cannon complete, a barrel on a wooden carriage with wheels, one carriage per barrel; the barrel raised to about 45 degrees so it points up over the wall toward the other castle; the two castles the same style and not mirror images; the tile grid parallel to the castle walls; no text in the picture; soft painterly colours.

Image 1 is the picks sheet, six candidates labelled A to F. A, B and C are new paintings, D asked for a side-by-side layout and did not draw it, E is the same request as B made by a second model, F is an earlier painting with one cannon repaired by inpainting.
Image 2 shows the cannons of A, B, C and E cropped at twice the size, the near castle and the far castle of each.

Judge the cannons from image 2, the crops. Answer in this order, short and concrete:
1. Per candidate A, B, C, E, from the crops: how many cannons in each castle, where each stands, whether each is complete with one carriage, whether the two in a castle are the same size, the barrel's elevation and where it points relative to the other castle. Name every cannon that breaks a rule.
2. Per candidate A to F, from the sheet: whether the castles are mirror images, whether the grid runs parallel to the walls, whether there is any text, anything else that is wrong or not the game's theme.
3. Rank the six, correctness of the cannons first, the grid second, the picture third, one sentence each. End with one line that starts with RANKING: followed by the six letters in order.
