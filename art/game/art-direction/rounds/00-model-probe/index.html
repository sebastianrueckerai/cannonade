# Round 00 notes: model probe

One neutral direction, three frames, five models, 15 images, 1.05 USD. Nano-banana first failed on `resolution: 1k`, the API wants `1K`.

Result, written into `docs/providers.md`:

- World and asset frames: nano-banana. The only model that kept the tile grid and the flat-shaded low-poly look in the world frame.
- Menu and UI frames: ideogram. Perfect text, clean low poly. Its world frame came back as an isolated asset on a transparent background.
- Cheap breadth: recraft flash at 0.007 USD. Clean low poly, perfect text, abstract world.
- Not for this: flux-3 paints, qwen renders textured 3D and garbles button text.

Gemini's verdict matched the director's read on every model.

Critiques

gemini (google/gemini-2.5-pro)
Here is the evaluation of the image models.

### flux3
1.  **Follows the prompt:** 4/5 - It follows most prompt elements well, but the world frame lacks a clear tile grid.
2.  **Low-poly game-art look:** 2/5 - The assets have a detailed, painterly look with soft lighting, not the requested flat-shaded, low-poly style.
3.  **Legible text in the menu frame:** 5/5 - The title and all four button labels are perfectly rendered and legible.
4.  **Consistency across the three frames:** 4/5 - The world and cannon frames share a detailed, painterly style, and the menu's background image matches this aesthetic.

### ideogram
1.  **Follows the prompt:** 3/5 - The world frame is an asset on a transparent background rather than a scene, and the camera angle is not as steep as requested.
2.  **Low-poly game-art look:** 4/5 - The cannon and world assets have a clean, low-poly appearance with simple shading.
3.  **Legible text in the menu frame:** 5/5 - "GRID CANNONS" is perfectly legible, as are all the button labels.
4.  **Consistency across the three frames:** 4/5 - The cannon and world assets share a consistent low-poly style, which is also reflected in the menu's background art.

### nanobanana
1.  **Follows the prompt:** 5/5 - The world frame clearly shows a fortress on a tile grid, the camera pitch is steep, the cannon is isolated, and the menu is correct.
2.  **Low-poly game-art look:** 5/5 - All three frames perfectly capture a low-poly, flat-shaded aesthetic with clean silhouettes.
3.  **Legible text in the menu frame:** 2/5 - The title is mostly legible but garbled ("GRID CANNON"), and the button text is completely unreadable.
4.  **Consistency across the three frames:** 5/5 - The three frames are perfectly consistent and look like they are from the exact same game.

### qwen
1.  **Follows the prompt:** 4/5 - It follows the prompts well, though the world frame's tile grid is more organic and less square than specified.
2.  **Low-poly game-art look:** 3/5 - The style is more "stylized 3D render" than "low-poly," with detailed textures and lighting.
3.  **Legible text in the menu frame:** 4/5 - The title "GRID CANNONS" is excellent, but the button text is gibberish, though it does add icons.
4.  **Consistency across the three frames:** 4/5 - The cannon and world frames are stylistically very consistent, creating a cohesive look for the game assets.

### recraft
1.  **Follows the prompt:** 4/5 - The world frame's camera angle is very steep and on a grid, but the fortress itself is abstract and unfinished.
2.  **Low-poly game-art look:** 5/5 - The model produces an excellent, clean, low-poly style with crisp edges and flat shading.
3.  **Legible text in the menu frame:** 5/5 - The title and all button labels are perfectly rendered and legible.
4.  **Consistency across the three frames:** 3/5 - While the cannon and world share a low-poly style, the world frame is too abstract to feel like it belongs with the finished cannon.

**Which model for the world and cannon frames?** `nanobanana`
**Which model for the menu frame?** `ideogram`
**Which model is the cheap one to use for breadth?** `flux3`

VERDICT: Use `nanobanana` for asset generation and `ideogram` for UI mockups, as no single model excels at both.
