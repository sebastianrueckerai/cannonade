You are an independent critic for a low-poly browser strategy game in the spirit of Rampart. The world, the palette and the shapes are decided: a painted toy fortress on a tile grid, cheerful, chunky, sunlit, cream stone, orange roofs, green grass, yellow sand, blue sea. This is the lighting phase. Your job is faults and readability, not taste.

Image 1 is the picks sheet: the same world frame under six lightings, numbered 1 to 6: 1 noon, a high sun, short hard shadows, clear sky, no fog; 2 morning, a low sun from the left, long soft shadows, a light haze at the horizon; 3 afternoon, a sun from the front right at 45 degrees, medium shadows, warm light, cream fog at the horizon; 4 fogged, a noon sun with depth fog that thickens to cream at the far edge, the sea fading into it; 5 overcast, no sun, flat ambient light, no cast shadows; 6 evening, a low warm sun, long shadows, an orange sky.

Image 2 is the 13 pixel test: the six frames scaled so one tile is 25 pixels wide and a one metre wall 12 pixels tall, the game's default view, magnified four times without smoothing. At this size the game must show: every wall apart from the sand and the grass by its base shadow, the gate, the towers, the cannons inside, the four team colours on the bases, the tile grid.

Answer in this order, short and concrete:
1. For each of the six: what drifted from frame 1 that the prompt did not ask for (objects, colours of the palette, camera), and what reads worse at 13 pixels than frame 1 does. Name the number.
2. At 13 pixels: which lightings keep the wall apart from the sand path at its base, which lose it. Which lightings keep the tile grid visible on the grass. Which one makes the cannons inside the yard hardest to count.
3. The sea: in which frames does the water read as a tile kind of the board, flat and calm, and in which does it become a painting that competes with the island.
4. The fog: at which depth does the fog help, by separating the island from the edge, and at which does it eat the far tiles the player must still read.
5. Three rules for the lighting file, as numbers where you can: the sun's elevation and azimuth in degrees, the shadow strength as a fraction between 0 and 1, the fog start and end as a fraction of the board depth.
6. Rank the six for the game's default view, one sentence each, readability first, mood second. End with one line that starts with RANKING: followed by the six numbers in order.
